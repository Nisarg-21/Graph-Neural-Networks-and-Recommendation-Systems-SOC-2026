## Q1. Normalizing the Adjacency Matrix

### Part 1 — Adjacency and degree matrices

For the path graph 1 — 2 — 3:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
D = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Row $i$ of $A$ lists which nodes node $i$ connects to (a 1 marks an edge). $D$ is diagonal with each node's degree: nodes 1 and 3 have degree 1, node 2 has degree 2.

### Part 2 — Adding self-loops

$$
\tilde{A} = A + I = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\tilde{D} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Adding $I$ puts a 1 on each diagonal entry — a self-loop — so during aggregation a node keeps a contribution from its own features instead of only its neighbors'. Each degree in $\tilde{D}$ rises by 1.

### Part 3 — Symmetric normalization

$$\hat{A} = \tilde{D}^{-1/2}\, \tilde{A}\, \tilde{D}^{-1/2}$$

**Step 1** — the inverse square-root degree matrix:

$$
\tilde{D}^{-1/2} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/\sqrt{3} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 2** — left-multiply (scales row $i$ by $1/\sqrt{\tilde{d}_i}$):

$$
\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{2} & 0 \\ 1/\sqrt{3} & 1/\sqrt{3} & 1/\sqrt{3} \\ 0 & 1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 3** — right-multiply (scales column $j$ by $1/\sqrt{\tilde{d}_j}$):

$$
\hat{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/2 & 1/\sqrt{6} & 0 \\ 1/\sqrt{6} & 1/3 & 1/\sqrt{6} \\ 0 & 1/\sqrt{6} & 1/2 \end{bmatrix}
$$



### Part 4 — Conceptual

A message from neighbor $j$ is scaled by $1/\sqrt{\tilde{d}_j}$, so a high-degree neighbor is down-weighted relative to a low-degree one. This is sensible because a hub's message is spread across many edges and is therefore less specific to any single node; down-weighting it stops popular, highly-connected nodes from dominating every neighbor's representation.
